# Extract Authorization into its own service for availability, with its own copy of the cards

Authorization leaves the monolith as `authorization-service`, a Maven module in this repo with its own PostgreSQL container, and the Network's gRPC link moves with it. The force (ADR-0003) is availability and isolation: the Network declines any Charge not answered within 1 second (ADR-0010), so a platform redeploy, crash or busy Clearing import or Reconciliation must not decline Charges. Latency was the force we expected, but M4 measured a p99 of 35.6 ms on one run and 104.2 ms on another, the miss caused by the laptop disk's commit flushes, so latency alone did not justify the move.

To answer without the platform, the service keeps its own copy of every card it may authorize, fed by the platform's card events over Kafka, and publishes its approved and reversed Authorizations back over Kafka, so `clearing` and `reconciliation` read a local copy instead of calling the service per record. We rejected calling the platform for each Charge behind a circuit breaker, because a down platform would then decline every Charge anyway, the failure the extraction exists to prevent. The cost is eventual consistency: each side's copy trails the other's by Kafka's delay, and the design has to say what happens inside that gap.

Card status is split: the platform still issues, cancels, expires and clears cards, and the service alone decides whether a card moves between ACTIVE and AUTHORIZED, which the platform's `cards` mirrors from the service's events. Inside the gap, a card the platform expires while the service approves a Charge on it stays EXPIRED, and its Clearing takes the existing AFTER_HOLD_EXPIRED outcome and Break. The Payment saga waits for the service to confirm it holds a new card before sending the Remittance Advice, so a Supplier never receives a card the service would decline as unknown, and a Clearing File waits in the drop folder while the platform's copy of Authorizations lags behind Kafka.

The monolith's `authorization` module loses the Network link, the rules and every answer rather than running alongside the service, since the simulator's Network is the only caller and local data is disposable. What stays is a thin module holding the platform's copy of approved and reversed Authorizations, fed by the service's events, so `clearing` and `reconciliation` keep reading it through `ApprovedAuthorizations` unchanged.
